Style direction

PickedA — Quiet & Trustworthy

A calorie tracker is a utility you open three to six times a day for years. Friendly/vibrant gets exhausting; the bold serif look reads editorial, not utilitarian. Direction A — muted neutrals, one deep-teal accent, soft 1px borders, no shadows except on the FAB and weight chart — respects the data density (numbers, lists, tables, sparklines), keeps the visual budget for what matters (the calories-remaining ring, the meal totals, the goal vs eaten delta), and ages well as the product layers in trends and history. The accent is reserved for primary actions and the "you are on track" signal — never decorative.

System

Type — Inter via Google Fonts. Scale: 11 (eyebrow), 13 (meta), 14–15 (body), 17 (section header), 22 (page title), 32+ (hero number). Tabular-nums on every number.

Grid — 4 px base; spacing units 4 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 20 / 24 / 32. Corner radii 8 / 12 / 14 / 18 / 28 (pill).

Borders — 1 px #E6E5E0 on cards. Shadows only on the FAB and the weight chart area gradient. Everything else is flat.

Touch — every actionable row is ≥ 44 px tall. Stepper buttons are 32 × 32 in a 48 px container (still ≥ 44 hit slop). Bottom-tab tap targets are 84 px tall.

Iconography — 1.6 stroke, round caps, inline SVG only. No filled glyphs.

Design notes (decisions for sign-off)

  1. Recents + Frequents live as chips above the results list, not on a separate tab. The PM brief flagged this as the single feature that makes daily logging tolerable. Putting them on a tab buries them. Chips are tappable from rest position and the results query results render below uninterrupted. On desktop they live in the right rail of the home view too — Quick add without leaving the day.
  2. The synthetic 100 g serving is shown explicitly, labeled "Synthetic". Per the spec, every food gets a 100 g serving at ingest so raw-grams logging stays in the single API shape. Power users will look for it; the label avoids surprising them when they see two yogurt servings of similar weight. Open question: do we hide it when an OFF-derived default exists? Recommend keeping it visible.
  3. FAB is placed bottom-right, not bottom-center. Bottom-center on a 390 wide phone collides with the iOS home-indicator gesture region and obscures the snack meal totals. Right-handed reachability is fine on this height. If usability testing shows lefties suffering, we move to bottom-center and shift meal totals up 16 px.
  4. The "calories remaining" ring is the only place we use color saturation on the home view. Macro bars use muted earth tones (protein/carbs/fat) so they read as data, not as alarms. The accent teal is reserved for "active state" and primary actions. When a macro is over budget we'll switch its bar to the danger color (#B5552E) — not shown in this mock; flag this for the engineering handoff.
  5. The log-entry sheet shows the predicted nutrition before save, in an accented preview block. This is the "will log" state. Users overcount when they can't see what their quantity choice translates to. Calculating it client-side from the food's per-100 g values × serving grams × quantity is trivial; the snapshot the server stores still wins on save. Worth committing to the API shape: detail response must return per-100 g nutrition (it already does).
